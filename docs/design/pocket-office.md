### Implementation notes: controls, materials and game feel

Presentation uses two visual native radio cards in both the toolbar and App Settings. Office exposes three atlas palette previews; Graph styles and customisation appear only in Graph, and their saved values survive an Office round trip. Scope is explicitly app-wide. The toolbar announces the actual save result. Graph retains its sticky sample; Office uses a centered, inert, deliberately composed two-times-scale room vignette with an example person, workstation, lounge, shelf and window.

Nameplates, room labels, message captions and selected-person inspector headers use scoped square paper-and-ink frames: warm paper and wood for Color, paper and charcoal for Neutral, mint and dark green for Green. The normal application font remains. Material tokens exist in dark, light and system blocks and do not recolor Graph, transcript fields or unrelated dialogs. The Team role editor is its existing inline inspector; Back to office clears the role selection and retains Catalog access. Existing shared native dialogs retain their semantics and behavior; this pass introduces no separate portal editor. Live Back and Escape return to a visible person, its directory entry when off-floor, or the named scene. Full Office messages live in a bounded, keyboard-reachable inspector scroller.

The motion audit found fixed-two-second traversal, a first-delivery-only renderer and abrupt desk returns. A pure journey sampler now uses 120 ms per logical tile with integer source-pixel positions, segment-facing walk poses, a 240 ms arrival pause and the same safe route home. A journey exceeding ten seconds stays a truthful static caption rather than rushing or cutting its path short. Admitted active journeys can finish within that limit; queued records still expire after eight seconds. At most three journeys may reserve disjoint endpoints and full two-by-three-worker corridors; up to twelve queued records coalesce same-endpoint bursts. Busy, distant, blocked or cross-floor messages retain immediate captions. A focused person freezes at its current point, cancels intersecting decoration and does not jump or replay on blur. Geometry changes rebase the focused target to its new desk and consume cancelled messages. Removed-agent maps are pruned.

One shared animation clock runs only for active visible Live deliveries. Reduced motion stops traversal immediately; hiding, resizing, floor/zoom changes, run switches and obsolete endpoints never replay retained messages. There are no idle loops or camera animations. Selection and press feedback are instant; a small static pixel mark accompanies the actual arrival pause. A stable named message strip keeps captions from resizing the stage and cancelling their own journeys. Visual delivery illustrates an already delivered message and never gates the runtime.

## Furnished office pass

A compact optional **Agents and history** disclosure provides current, finished, overflow and earlier-session inspection, with session and department context and access to unloaded history. It starts closed and does not consume scene height until opened. Keep the existing style entry and inspector; this pass adds no screen or setting.

Build coherent workplace vignettes rather than one desk in a large empty rectangle. Size departments for their occupants, join small teams on a floor where feasible, and fill shared space with purposeful furnishings: wood desks with screens, paper and keyboards; bookcases and cabinets against walls; windows and diagram boards; a lounge with sofa, side table and lamp; a coffee and printer service corner; plants marking room corners. Maintain a continuous central aisle and door openings. Plain floor provides breathing room between detailed objects; avoid decorative objects strewn over routes. Vacant desk capacity must not dominate the composition. Furnishings respond to occupancy and available space, not only the outer perimeter.

At the default view people should read as humans, preferably at a 2x integer sprite scale where room fit permits. Each of twelve identities combines recognizable hair, skin, clothing construction and body shape: cropped hair with blue workwear; side-part and glasses with pink shirt; bun and green tunic; broad shoulders and striped ochre top; long hair and violet jacket; cap and teal workwear; rounded afro and ochre pullover with cream trim; ponytail and blue jacket; bob and belted rose dress; bald head with beard and green shirt; segmented braid and teal overalls; and gray hair with brown vest over cream sleeves. ## Building composition

Each actual direct child of the root defines a department; its descendants share that department. Label it using the actual lead name, with explicit prior-session context where applicable; the root occupies a central area. Link rooms with visible wall openings and walkable corridors. Overflow uses numbered rooms or floors; same-floor messages can walk through corridors, while cross-floor messages get an immediate labeled cue without a fictional walk. Make counts and floor navigation explicit; never invent reporting relationships. Rooms and corridor bounds respond to the available canvas and fill it rather than leaving a fixed small room in one corner. Furniture and aisle layout adapt to size while collision and route connectivity remain valid. Keep readable, normal-size department labels and worker names/status overlays independent of sprite zoom, with visible selection and focus; prevent labels from obscuring doors, paths or controls. Maintain accessible roster and inspector access at every size. The [building concept](../../src/renderer/src/assets/pocket-office/review-color-building.svg) shows intended spatial relationships; its names and agents are illustrative sample data.

Worker frames are 16 × 24 with feet pivot `[8,23]`. Stable names are `worker.<silhouette>.<direction>.<pose>`: directions `down`, `left`, `right`, `up`; poses `idle`, `walk-1`, `walk-2`, `work`, `wait`, `done`, `error`. Metadata lists all 336 supported names explicitly, even where static poses share a rectangle. Twelve silhouettes are assigned stably by agent ID, never by state or color: `crop`, `part`, `bun`, `wide`, `long`, `cap`, `afro`, `ponytail`, `bob`, `bald`, `braid`, `vest`. There is no idle animation.

Each prop record specifies `frame`, `placement`, `footprint`, `interactionAnchors`, and `depthAnchor`. The footprint lists blocked grid-cell offsets from the placement origin. Each interaction anchor names an adjacent unblocked walkable cell offset and a facing; desk anchors must connect to an aisle. The depth anchor is an integer pixel position in the prop. Sort objects by feet/depth y, with foreground pieces above workers separately. `crt`, `crt-side`, and `mug` are tabletop details: layer them over a supporting desk and do not add a second floor obstacle. All other props use the conservative 2 × 2 footprint listed in metadata.

Tile ID families: `tile.00`–`.07` floor (00 plain, 01 horizontal seam, 02 vertical seam, 03 both, 04–07 sparse marks); `.08`–`.15` wall (08 straight, 09 left edge, 10 right edge, 11 both, 12–15 repeat those variants); `.16`–`.23` rugs (16 plain border, 17 inset, 18 stripe, 19 inset and stripe, 20–23 repeat); `.24`–`.31` doors (24 plain, 25 upper panel, alternating); `.32`–`.39` partitions (32 plain, 33 center post, alternating); `.40`–`.47` floor details (40 solid, 41 inset, 42 stripe, 43 inset and stripe, then repeat). Prefer plain floor and sparse seams so furniture remains legible. Stable prop and worker IDs and crop rectangles are in `atlas.json`; import it and the selected palette's PNGs as assets, not inferred array offsets.
